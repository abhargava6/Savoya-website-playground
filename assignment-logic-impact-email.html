Methodology
We randomly selected 305 reservations from Feb–Apr 2026 across 5 major markets. Recreated them in pre-prod (mirrored prod data + new code) as August trips with no special events. Based on historical data, the second-ranked vendor on average ends up servicing the trip, so we use second-pick quotes as the basis for this analysis rather than always taking first, third, etc.
● Sample: 305 trips (~1% of annual market volume)
● Markets: NYC, SF, DFW, LA, Chicago
● Score balance factor: 1.5 (prev. 0 or 0.5)
● Measures second vendor pick savings
● Does not capture: manual overrides
● Annual revenue: ~$60M, margin: ~49%

Both paths deliver meaningful savings. The reason both paths show strong results is the per-service-area score balance factor (AKA margin optimization factor), set to 1.5 in these markets. This factor penalizes vendors whose rates are disproportionately high relative to their quality score. Increasing the factor to 2.0 would further increase savings under the quality-optimized path. Savings are positive across every discount bracket.
Score Balance Factor (set to 1.5)
Previously set at 0 or 0.5, the score balance factor at 1.5 filters out vendors who score well but charge unreasonably high rates. On quality-optimized trips, the algorithm agrees with the price-optimized path 45.7% of the time — it naturally avoids overpaying.
● When the two paths diverge (54.3% of trips): picks vendors with avg score 20.97 vs. 17.18 at a modest $37/trip premium
● That premium buys meaningfully better quality and margin for Savoya

Also Shipped
Features Beyond the Simulation
The simulation measures vendor pick savings only. These features are live and deliver impact that isn’t captured in the numbers above.
◆ Costless Vendor Removal
Vendors with no cost data are excluded from auto-assignment entirely. Previously, these vendors could be assigned trips at unknown cost. They’re now flagged in the Reassign Modal so ops can proactively add their rates — converting them from a blind risk into a known, competing vendor.
◆ Unavailable Bucket in Reassign Modal
The redesigned modal now shows ALL vendors in a market across four buckets: Available, Available-No-Auto, Low Margin, and Unavailable. Ops can scan the full market in seconds instead of checking vendors individually. Also surfaces why a vendor is unavailable — enabling proactive capacity planning.
◆ Beta Vendor Types (Infrastructure live, not yet enabled)
New Beta Garage and Beta I/O vendor types are in the system, gated by the Beta Eligibility Flag. When enabled, these vendors enter the auto-assign pool — directly increasing supply in every market. More supply means more competition and better pricing. Enabling in 2–3 pilot markets is a configuration change, not a code deploy.
◆ Custom Rate Discount Evaluation
The system now uses effective discount = max(explicit discount, custom rate discount) to determine routing. Previously, a client with a 5% explicit discount but a 40% custom rate discount would be quality-optimized — the wrong path. Now they’re correctly routed to price-optimized.
◆ Assignment Audit Trail
Every assignment now logs: score balance factor used, effective discount, decision path summary, and a 23-step vendor filter funnel with counts. This turns optimization from guesswork into a data-driven feedback loop — when a market underperforms, we can see exactly why.
ⓘ Feature Flag Control
Vendor Offering Feature Flag — TC and PAX Level
The Vendor Offering Feature Flag now supports three options: “Trip effective discount decides Low cost or Best score” (default), “Always prioritize low cost vendors”, or “Always prioritize best score vendors”. Configurable at Company, TC, and PAX level.
This gives sales and account management precise control over client routing. Premium clients can be set to “Always prioritize best score vendors” to ensure the highest-quality vendor match regardless of discount level. The infrastructure is live and ready for configuration.
 
Recommended Next Steps
Where We Go From Here
1
Evaluate and set score balance factors per market
Vendor Reporting
The 1.5 score balance factor shows strong projections in high-volume markets, but we still need to evaluate and decide what to set in each individual market to maximize return. To do this, we need to pull reports on vendor score and cost in each market and gauge the right factor. DFW is a clear starting point — a single high-cost/high-score vendor (Dauntless) is suppressing savings there.
2
Audit Savoya Preferred and Client Preferred vendors
Vendor CSM Sales
Worth reviewing the Savoya Preferred list for vendors whose costs are disproportionately high or who no longer warrant the designation. For high-cost vendors worth retaining, rate renegotiation could unlock meaningful savings. For Client Preferred vendors, the top vendors by volume have incentive to offer better pricing — a good starting point for conversations.
3
Configure the Vendor Offering Feature Flag for key clients
CSM Sales
Short term: Identify select passengers who should always receive the best vendors (including preferred or highest-score vendors) and set their flag to “Always prioritize best score vendors.”
Long term: Begin identifying which clients should receive this treatment at the Company or TC level. If teams want to enable it for their clients, they can make the case.
4
Train team on using the Unavailable bucket for faster fulfilment
Ops
The Unavailable bucket in the reassign modal contains vendors excluded from auto-assignment for specific reasons — vehicle type mismatch, Groundwork-only, etc. These vendors can be manually assigned to find fulfilment faster, but proper due diligence is needed before sending a trip their way. The team should understand when it’s appropriate to reach into this bucket and what to verify before doing so.
5
Establish a 30-day review cadence
Reporting
The solution provides the data to monitor per-market performance. A recurring report could flag markets with high negative-savings rates, vendors consistently chosen at above-market cost, and flag override patterns worth reviewing.
6
Add costs for flagged costless vendors
Vendor
The reassign modal now surfaces these vendors with a flag. Getting their rates into the system converts them from excluded to competing — increasing supply and competitive pressure in their markets.
7
Consider enabling Beta vendors in pilot markets
Leadership
Markets with limited vendor supply or high concentration in a single vendor would benefit most. The infrastructure is ready — this is a configuration change, not a code deploy.

Important Caveats
● The 18% is based on second-vendor pick quotes. Based on historical data, the second-ranked vendor on average ends up servicing the trip — more realistic than best-case first-pick numbers.
● Conservative projection uses a 50% realization factor to account for manual overrides, market dynamics, and sales configuring the Vendor Offering Feature Flag for premium clients.
● Sample is 305 trips with second-pick data from a ~35K annual pool. Directionally strong but not statistically definitive at market level.
● Score balance factor of 1.5 was used; increasing to 2.0 per market would further increase quality-optimized path savings.
● Savoya Preferred vendors were not considered in this simulation — trip offers went to vendors without factoring in preference status. Actual results will differ where preferred vendors are assigned.
● Savings apply only to trips booked after go-live. Already booked and assigned trips will not be affected by the new routing logic.
● Every change is toggleable via feature flags per Company/TC/PAX. No code deploy needed to adjust.
