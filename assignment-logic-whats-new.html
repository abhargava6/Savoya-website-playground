Context

Why we made these changes

💵

Better Margin Decisions

Previously, the system ranked vendors using rough estimates — not actual quotes. The vendor that looked cheapest wasn't always cheapest when the real invoice landed. Now, ranking uses real vendor quotes.

🏁

Beta Vendors Getting Volume

Our newer "Beta" vendors (formerly called ASAP) were completely excluded from auto-assignment. They now participate in the pool where appropriate — increasing supply and competition.

🎛️

Per-Client Routing Controls

There was no easy way to route a specific client differently. Now, Sales and Ops can configure routing at the Company, TC, or Passenger level — independently.

🔍

Visibility Into Decisions

When a vendor was auto-assigned, it was hard to know why. A new Audit Trail tab now shows every decision the system made — who was considered, who was filtered out, and why the winner was chosen.

Projected outcome: Conservative estimate of ~$1.0M in annual savings across analyzed markets — from smarter routing, accurate cost comparisons, and competitive vendor pools. See the Impact slide for full breakdown.

Before

How assignment worked previously

The previous logic had two main paths — retail and non-retail — but used rough cost estimates and excluded Beta vendors entirely. This is the baseline the new system replaces.

Now

How assignment works today

The new logic adds a smarter non-retail split (premium vs cost path), brings Beta vendors into the pool, refines retail routing by email domain, and uses real vendor quotes throughout.

Change 1 of 5

Two routing paths — matched to client type

Every non-retail trip now follows one of two assignment strategies, based on how price-sensitive the client is. The dividing line is the client's effective discount — calculated as the higher of their stated discount or the implicit discount their custom rate represents against the standard rack rate.

Quality-Optimized Path

For clients with < 30% discount

The system prioritizes quality vendors first — TC Preferred, then Savoya Preferred — then finds the lowest-cost option within that quality band.


Vendor order: TC Preferred → Savoya Preferred → Best quality/cost balance


Who this is: Most business clients — matched with reliable, high-scoring vendors.

Price-Optimized Path

For clients with ≥ 30% discount

The system skips preferred vendors entirely and finds the lowest-cost eligible vendor, ranked by actual vendor quote.


Vendor order: All eligible vendors, ranked cheapest first. No preference tiers.


Who this is: Price-sensitive accounts. Lower margin, so we optimize for cost to protect the trip.

Override available (Admin & Manager roles only): The routing path can be overridden at the Company, TC, or Passenger level using a 3-option dropdown: "Default" (discount decides), "Always prioritize low cost vendors", or "Always prioritize best score vendors." Sales/CSM cannot modify this setting directly.

Change 2 of 5

Real vendor quotes now drive ranking

Previously, the assignment engine compared vendors using a simplified base rate — not the actual quote the vendor would receive. This caused mismatches between which vendor looked cheapest and which actually was cheapest.

Now, the ranking cost is built from the actual vendor quote, including all cost components.

✅ Base rate

Already included before — no change here.

New Trial discount

Vendors in trial mode get a configurable discount applied during ranking — so trial vendors compete fairly on actual cost.

New Fuel surcharge

Each vendor's fuel surcharge is now factored into ranking, not just the final invoice.

New Garage travel time

Time billed for the vendor driving to and from their garage is now included in the ranking cost.

What does this mean in practice?

The vendor selected by auto-assign may be different than what the old logic would have picked — even for the same trip.

The "right" vendor is now the one with the lowest total real cost, not the lowest base rate. This means more accurate margin calculations and fewer surprises on invoices.

You can verify this in the Audit Trail tab — it shows exactly what cost was used to rank each vendor.

Custom rate clients Updated

Clients on custom pricing are now evaluated differently. The system calculates what effective discount their custom rate represents and uses the higher of that or their explicit discount to determine which path they follow. A client with a 5% explicit discount but a 40% custom rate discount will now correctly route to the price-optimized path.

Change 3 of 5

Beta vendors now participate in auto-assignment New vendor types

Vendors previously labeled "ASAP Garage" have been renamed to Beta Garage. A new type, Beta I/O, has also been introduced. These vendors now participate in auto-assignment — a major change from before.

Beta Garage

Renamed from "ASAP Garage." Behaves identically to a standard Garage in scoring, robocalling, and communications. The only difference: assignment eligibility is now toggled per client.

Beta I/O

New vendor type. Behaves like a standard Independent Operator. Same scoring, same communications. Eligibility controlled by the same per-client toggle.

Per-client toggle (Beta Eligibility): Whether Beta vendors appear in auto-assignment is controlled at the Company, TC, and Passenger level. Admin/Manager roles only can change this. No Beta vendors are enabled at the moment — we will gradually onboard them into the auto-assignment pool market by market.

What you'll see

  • → Beta vendors appearing in the "Available for Auto-assignment" bucket in the Reassign modal
  • → If Beta is disabled for a client, these vendors appear in "Available (No Auto-assignment)" with reason: "Beta vendor not enabled for this client"
  • → Beta vendors competing on cost or quality alongside standard vendors — no separate bucket
  • → The old "ASAP" bucket in the Reassign modal is gone

Note for Vendor Ops: Existing ASAP Garage vendors were renamed automatically. No vendor re-enrollment needed. Scoring, communications, and robocall behavior are unchanged.

Change 4 of 5

Retail trips now route smarter

Retail clients (those who booked through the consumer portal) are now treated differently based on their email domain and how many trips they've taken.

🏢

Business email · First 3 trips

Clients who signed up with a corporate email (not Gmail, Yahoo, etc.) get quality-optimized routing for their first 3 trips — to capture value from potentially high-value business accounts.


Preferred vendors: Excluded. Best quality/cost match selected.

🔄

Business email · 4th trip onward

After 3 completed trips on file, business-email retail clients move permanently to price-optimized routing — same as personal email clients.


Preferred vendors: Excluded. Beta vendors: Controlled by toggle.

📧

Personal email · All trips

Clients with Gmail, Yahoo, iCloud, Outlook, and other personal email domains always route price-optimized. No trial window. No preferred vendors in the pool.


Always price-optimized from trip 1. No exceptions.

Conversion rule: If a retail TC is later linked to a Company (converted to non-retail), all new trips from that point follow the standard non-retail logic. Trips already booked before conversion are unaffected.

Change 5 of 5

Vendors without pricing are excluded from auto-assign

Previously, a vendor with no cost on file could still win auto-assignment — they'd just appear at the end of the list. This led to assignments where we didn't know what the trip would cost until after it was confirmed.

Now, any vendor without pricing data on file is completely excluded from auto-assignment — on both the quality-optimized and the price-optimized paths.

What happens to these vendors?

They don't disappear — they show up in the Reassign modal under "Available (No Auto-assignment)" with the reason: No cost on file.

Ops can still manually assign them from there if needed — the system just won't auto-assign them. This also surfaces which vendors need rate entry — converting a risk into a known quantity.

Also excluded from the Price-Optimized Path

Preferred vendors (both Savoya Preferred and Client Preferred) are excluded when a trip routes through the price-optimized path. This is intentional — on this path we're optimizing for lowest price, not preference.

These vendors appear in "Available (No Auto-assignment)" in the modal with reason: Preferred — skipped.

Why this matters: Even one costless vendor assignment per week at an inflated rate could offset thousands in algorithm savings. This is a risk-elimination feature.

New in the UI

Redesigned Reassign Vendor Modal

The biggest day-to-day UI change for ops. The modal now has four clear buckets, more vendor detail, and explicit reasons for every exclusion.

✅ Available for Auto-assignment

Vendors the system would actually send this trip to, listed in the exact order they'd be offered. Includes vendors whose offer was declined or expired — they stay here with their offer status shown.

⚠ Available — No Auto-assignment

Vendors that could serve this trip but are being deliberately skipped by the new rules. Each one shows a reason:
"No cost on file" · "Beta vendor not enabled for this client" · "Preferred — skipped"
Ops can still manually assign from here.

📉 Low Margin

Same as before — vendors that exceed the 90% margin ceiling. No change to this bucket.

🚫 Unavailable

Vendors that fail a genuine filter — vehicle type mismatch, blackout, conflicting trip, vaccination requirements, etc. Now expanded to show all active vendors in the service area, with a plain-English reason for each exclusion.

New columns in the modal

💰 Vendor Quote 📊 Score 💵 Algo Cost 🔢 Scoring Detail ⭐ Preference (SV / CP) 📋 Decline Reason ❓ Exclusion Reason 🔗 Vendor Profile Link
Key change: The old "ASAP" bucket is gone. Beta vendors are now part of the main assignment pool. The "Decline Reason" column (Offer Sent / Declined / Expired) is separate from the "Reason" column (why a vendor is excluded) — these mean different things.

New in the UI

Vendor Assignment Trail

A new tab on the reservation page — "VENDOR ASSIGNMENT TRAIL" — that logs every decision the system made for each assignment event. Visible to Admin and Manager roles only.

👤

Client Section

Shows: TC name, Retail or Non-Retail status, discount level applied, and which routing path (quality-optimized or price-optimized) was used for this trip.

🔽

Filter Funnel

Step-by-step view of how the vendor pool was narrowed — how many vendors passed each filter stage and which specific vendors were removed at each step.

📋

Eligible Vendors

Full ranked list of vendors that made it through all filters — with their score, cost used for ranking, and actual quote. The winning vendor is highlighted.

🧭

Decision Path

Bullet list of each logic decision: which path was taken, whether preferred vendors were matched, and what triggered the routing choice.

💲

Client Quote

The client-facing quote at assignment time — base rate, discount, margin threshold, and adjusted total.

🏆

Result

The final outcome: which vendor was assigned, their score, and the cost that drove the decision.

Each assignment and reassignment creates a separate entry in the trail, so you can see the full history of a reservation's vendor decisions in one place. The logging feature flag is currently off by default and enabled on-demand to avoid performance impact.

Safety Net

Every change is behind a feature flag

None of these changes are all-or-nothing. Each piece of logic is controlled by a feature flag — a toggle that can be turned on or off per client, per market, or globally — without a code deployment.

Vendor Offering Override

The 3-option dropdown (Quality-Optimized / Price-Optimized / Default) at Company/TC/Passenger is independently configurable. If a specific account needs to be locked to one path, this can be set without affecting other accounts.

Beta Vendor Eligibility

Beta vendors can be toggled off per Company/TC/Passenger. If a client shouldn't see Beta vendors, flip the switch. Default is ON for all new clients.

Retail First-3-Trip Window

The logic that gives business-email retail clients quality-optimized routing for their first 3 trips is controlled separately and can be toggled without affecting corporate clients.

Launch defaults: At go-live, all routing overrides are set to "Default" (discount-based logic applies for everyone). No accounts are forced onto a new path immediately — behavior changes gradually as the system uses real quotes. This gives us a clean baseline to measure from.

For Your Team

What this means — by role

Sales & CSM

  • Premium clients are now matched with higher-quality vendors by default — no manual intervention needed
  • The Vendor Offering override lets you request specific routing for key accounts (Admin/Manager sets it)
  • Clients on custom rates (e.g., Bridgewater) may now route differently — the system calculates their effective discount against the standard rack rate, and uses the higher of that or their explicit discount. This means some custom-rate clients will shift to the price-optimized path
  • The audit trail provides backup when clients ask "why this vendor?"

Operations & Coordinators

  • The Reassign modal now shows why each vendor was excluded — no more guessing
  • The Unavailable bucket shows all vendors in the market, enabling faster fulfilment when the top pick declines
  • Costless vendors are flagged — reach out to get their rates entered to increase the eligible pool
  • Beta vendors now compete alongside standard vendors — the old ASAP bucket is gone

Leadership & Management

  • Conservative estimate: ~$1.0M annual savings across 5 analyzed markets (36.7% of total volume)
  • Every change is feature-flagged — rollback is per-client, not all-or-nothing
  • The rate balance factor is a new per-market lever for tuning vendor cost vs. quality tradeoffs
  • 30-day review cadence recommended to monitor per-market performance and optimize settings

Vendor Team

  • Existing ASAP vendors were renamed to Beta automatically — no re-enrollment needed
  • Costless vendors are now surfaced in the Reassign modal — opportunity to get rates entered and increase their trip volume
  • Beta vendors entering the pool means more competition in each market — monitor pricing trends
  • Preferred vendor lists are worth reviewing: high-cost preferred vendors reduce algorithm savings

Reporting & Finance

  • The audit trail creates a data source for per-market, per-vendor cost analysis
  • Rate balance factor tuning (per market) is the primary lever for optimizing quality vs. cost tradeoff
  • A 30-day review cadence is recommended: flag markets with high negative-savings rates, vendors consistently above market cost, and override patterns
  • Savings are positive across every discount bracket (0% through 30%+) — the algorithm works regardless of client pricing tier
