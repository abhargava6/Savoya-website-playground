How Savoya works today

Savoya is a managed marketplace, not a fleet

We hold the client relationship, the rates and the quality bar. A vendor supplies the vehicle and the driver. Worth a minute because it explains two things later.

Nearly everything confirms immediately. We accept the booking and find the vendor in the background. A small set of conditions holds at pending until a vendor accepts — that is the only reason you will ever see a pending car.

Chauffeur details arrive later. Name, phone and plate do not exist at booking time. They appear when the vendor assigns a driver, which is why there is a separate driver-assigned event.

How pricing works

There is no trip classification. Every trip is just a trip — what varies is which rate applies, and we work that out from the route.

Transfer rate

Point-to-point flat rate, and generally the cheaper option. Route-specific — we configure them where the economics work. We have them for most airport-to-city transfers, which is exactly your traffic.

Hourly rate

Time-based, with minimums — a 30-minute run against a two-hour minimum prices at the minimum. Applies wherever no transfer rate exists.

You do not tell us which to use. There is no hourly-or-transfer field to send. We pick it from the route.

Adding a stop changes the rate. A transfer rate is priced point-to-point; add an intermediate stop and it falls back to hourly. Airport → office → hotel is a normal pattern, so this will come up.

Rates are per company. Rack rate by default. Negotiated rates come from a conversation with our sales during onboarding — which is the real reason a flight department connects.

When a booking does not confirm straight away

Four conditions. Everything else confirms immediately.

A confirmed trip can go back to pending. If it is a limited-availability trip and it gets modified, the vendor has to re-accept.

A late edit can push an ordinary trip into pending — because the edit itself brings it inside the pickup cutoff. Your users re-time flights late, and late is exactly when this triggers. Worth expecting rather than treating as a fault.

This also answers your question six. Coverage, lead time and vehicle availability are not a static rule set you would hold a copy of. They are evaluated per reservation, and the pricing call is where you find out.

Driver assigned. Your lifecycle goes Confirmed straight to Driver En Route, but your original sketch had a dispatched state. We assign a driver some time before they set off, and that is when chauffeur name, phone and plate become real. Enough as Confirmed-with-details, or would you rather have the state?

Two that sit between your states. Circling — the driver is at the airport but moving, going round the terminal because they cannot wait at the kerb. Very common at FBOs. Driver waiting — the trip is underway and the car is holding at an intermediate stop. We would fold the first into At Location and the second into Driving, but neither is exact. Tell us if you would rather have them.

Two of yours have no home here. Pending Cancelation — our cancel is a flag, immediate, with no workflow. And Rejected — when no vendor can be found we cancel and notify, so it would reach you as a cancellation carrying a reason. Does that work?

Statuses can run backwards. Onsite, onboard, closeout and billing can all roll back — a billed trip can be corrected and re-billed. Because every event is a full snapshot and you upsert, your handler needs no special case. It only matters where you have copied a price onto an expense record.

Price, then book

None of this exists yet — it is the shape we would propose. The pricing call would double as the availability check, so you hold no copy of our rules. Tell us if it does not fit how your users work.

Nothing exists on our side until the booking call. The pricing step returns a held price against a rate id, not a reservation — so a user can wander off and come back without leaving anything behind.

What we are proposingYour decision

Getting a flight department set up

Sign-up is only mandatory for direct bill — retail card bookings need none. So the question is not whether, but when and who drives it.

Option 1 — cleanest, but you build a form. User never leaves your product and billing is right from booking one. If credit approval is needed we owe you a pending state.

Option 2 — nothing is ever blocked. Nothing for you to build, one context switch the first time only, and the first trip converts off the card onto direct bill.

Neither is obviously right — it turns on things only you know. How your customers react to leaving the product, and how much form-building you are willing to do.

What we have assumed about your side

This is the part we would most like you to pull apart. Everything below is a guess we have built on.

  • Flight department is the billing entity, with nothing above it — you told us this, and it settled an argument on our side
  • One flight department maps to one Savoya company, one to one, and stays that way
  • Riders can be sent as name, phone and email with no pre-registration — you asked for this and we can do it
  • Crew and passengers can both be riders and we do not need the distinction
  • Only airport-to-first-destination legs, not the trips in between
  • Time changes are the dominant modification
  • Sedan and SUV cover almost everything; groups above 14 are rare
  • You can adopt price-then-book rather than booking direct
  • You will adopt our field names and translate in your integration layer — as you offered
  • Direct bill to the flight department is acceptable, and no card data ever crosses to you

The one we are least sure of: that crew and passengers can be treated the same. Your base does both self-serve and booking-for-others — a pilot arranging his own hotel run is booker and rider at once. We support that, but tell us if the distinction needs to reach us for rating or reporting.

What we would like from you

Ten things, roughly in the order they block us.

01

The onboarding decision. Option 1 or Option 2 — and if Option 1, are you willing to build the form?

02

Does price-then-book actually work in your product? It assumes your UI can show vehicle types with prices and let the user pick. If it cannot, tell us now — we would need the vehicle types your product already knows, and a different shape.

03

Billing, and who we actually work with. If flight departments contract with us directly, someone has to be the counterparty for billing, invoices and credit. But any of your users can start a booking. Who is that person, and do you have an owner or admin concept — or are all users equal?

04

Show us your UI. Where would our booking live, what does the timeline look like around it, and how much of it can you shape for us? A walkthrough would answer more than another email exchange.

05

The status questions. Driver assigned, circling and driver waiting, and whether your Awaiting Confirmation covers our limited-availability case.

06

The booking types your customers actually use. You said partial coverage is what kills these integrations. We would rather check the list against the API now and tell you where the gaps are.

07

Master trip linking. You raised it on our first call and we still owe you an answer. Send an identifier tying legs to one trip and we can carry it and echo it back.

08

Notifications. We can turn ours off entirely if you would rather own it. The car detail already prints on your tripsheets — tell us what avoids telling the same person twice.

09

Rewards. You raised this first and you were right that it decides whether crews actually use this. We would like to brainstorm it with you.

10

Two small ones. How long your schedulers sit on a booking screen, since that sets how long we hold a price. And whether an airport change is a modify or a cancel-and-rebook.
